Table grains and unique keys (as implemented in the Gold aggregations):
  - gold_order_analytics, golden_order_summary: one row per order_id
  - gold_product_analytics: one row per product_id
  - gold_category_performance: one row per product_category_name (74 rows,
    73 named categories plus one NULL-category row)
  - gold_customer_analytics: one row per customer_unique_id
  - gold_seller_analytics: one row per seller_id
  - gold_seller_state_performance: one row per seller_state
  - monthly_orders: one row per (order_year, order_month)
  - silver_order_items: one row per (order_id, order_item_id)
Uniqueness therefore follows each table's own grain - order_id is the key of
the order-level tables only, not of every table. The substantive check that
no join has duplicated or lost value is the reconciliation of the Gold
revenue, freight and order-value totals (R$ 13,591,643.70 / R$ 2,251,909.54 /
R$ 15,843,553.24) against the silver_order_items source, which matches
exactly - not row-count equality alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEY METRIC DEFINITIONS (as implemented in the dashboard and Gold layer)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Total Revenue       = SUM(total_price): the product price of the items in an
                        order. EXCLUDES freight.
* Total Freight       = SUM(total_freight_value): the freight charged on the
                        items in an order. EXCLUDES product price.
* Total Order Value   = SUM(total_item_value) = product price + freight.
* Average Order Value = AVG(total_item_value) = average order value INCLUDING
                        freight, across orders that have item records.
* Revenue, freight and order-value totals are computed only over the 98,666
  orders that have matching order-item records (see Section H for the 775
  orders without item data).
* Average Delivery Time = AVG(delivery_days), the difference in days between
  order_purchase_timestamp and order_delivered_customer_date, for delivered
  orders where this can be computed.

A. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

Between September 2016 and October 2018 the dataset records 99,441 orders,
of which 96,478 (97.02%) reached the customer with status 'delivered'.

  Total orders ................. 99,441
  Delivered orders ............. 96,478  (97.02% of all orders)
  Total revenue (excl. freight)  R$ 13,591,643.70
  Total freight ................. R$  2,251,909.54
  Total order value (incl. freight) R$ 15,843,553.24
  Average order value (incl. freight) R$ 160.58
  Average delivery time ........ 12.5 days

Note the deliberate distinction between the two monetary headline figures:

* Revenue (R$ 13,591,643.70) is product price only and EXCLUDES freight.
* Total order value (R$ 15,843,553.24) is product price plus freight; it equals
  revenue + freight exactly (13,591,643.70 + 2,251,909.54 = 15,843,553.24).

Freight accounts for R$ 2,251,909.54, or 14.21% of total order value - a
material share of what customers pay in total. The average order value of
R$ 160.58 includes freight because it is the average of per-order price plus
freight; the average product-only order value is correspondingly lower.

The operation is overwhelmingly a delivery-success story at first glance
(97.02% delivered), but Section C shows that roughly 1 in 12 delivered orders
arrived after the estimated delivery date, which is the more relevant
service-quality signal in this dataset.

C. DELIVERY PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

C.1 On-time versus late delivery
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Denominator (important): the classification covers DELIVERED orders that have
BOTH a customer delivery date and an estimated delivery date. Of the 96,478
delivered orders, 8 have no order_delivered_customer_date (none are missing
the estimated date). The classification denominator is therefore 96,470
delivered orders:

  On-time (delivered on or before the estimate): 88,644 orders  = 91.89%
  Late (delivered after the estimate):             7,826 orders  =  8.11%

An order is counted on time when order_delivered_customer_date <=
order_estimated_delivery_date. As a share of ALL 96,478 delivered orders, the
on-time rate is 91.88% (the 8 unclassified orders are less than 0.01% of the
total, so the two bases differ by only 0.01 percentage points).

C.2 Average delivery time
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Average delivery time is 12.5 days from purchase timestamp to customer
delivery, computed over the 96,470 delivered orders where delivery days can be
measured (it matches the dashboard KPI exactly).

C.3 Potential operational implications (interpretation, to be validated)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Roughly 1 in 12 delivered orders (8.11%) misses the promise date. If review
  scores were available, one could test whether late deliveries coincide with
  poor reviews - the review dataset exists in the raw source data but was not
  ingested in this pipeline, so this remains a hypothesis, not a finding.
* It is not yet clear whether lateness is concentrated in particular sellers,
  product categories, customer regions or carriers: seller and customer states
  are available, but geolocation and carrier data were not ingested.
* The 12.5-day average delivery is long relative to many e-commerce markets,
  but expected for Brazilian cross-state logistics; no benchmark claim is made
  here.

D.4 What the comparison indicates (interpretation)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* High-revenue and high-volume rankings overlap heavily (9 of 10 categories
  appear in both lists), but the order differs. cama_mesa_banho (bed, table
  and bath) leads units (11,115) at a low average price of R$ 93.30, while
  beleza_saude (health and beauty) leads revenue (R$ 1,258,681.34) on a
  higher average price of R$ 130.16.
* relogios_presentes (watches and gifts) is the clearest premium-volume case:
  it ranks 2nd by revenue (R$ 1,205,005.68) with only 5,991 units, at an
  average price of R$ 201.14 (also 10th by average price).
* telefonia sells 4,545 units (8th by volume) but only R$ 323,667.53 in
  revenue - high volume, low ticket, and absent from the revenue top 10.
* The highest average-price categories (pcs at R$ 1,098.34, computers;
  portateis_casa_forno_e_cafe at R$ 624.29) sell in small volumes (203 and 76
  units). They are niche, high-ticket categories rather than revenue drivers;
  pcs generates R$ 222,963.13 in total, far below the top categories.

For assortment and sales strategy this suggests two distinct roles in the
catalog: broad, low-ticket categories (cama_mesa_banho, utilidades_domesticas,
moveis_decoracao) drive traffic and units, while mid-to-high-ticket categories
(beleza_saude, relogios_presentes, informatica_acessorios) drive revenue.
This is an observation about the current mix; revenue is not profit - no cost
or margin data exists in this dataset, so no profitability conclusions are
drawn.

At product level (gold_product_analytics, 32,951 products, 112,650 units sold,
R$ 13,591,643.70 revenue), the top product (a beleza_saude item) contributes
R$ 63,885.00 from 195 units - well under 0.5% of revenue - showing that
category strength comes from many products rather than a single blockbuster.

only the measured counts.

================================================================================
G. KEY BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================
Each recommendation states what the data shows, why it matters, and what
could be investigated or done. None of these is a causal claim.

G.1 Investigate the 8.11% late-delivery segment before changing promises
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: 7,826 of 96,470 classified delivered orders arrived after the
  estimated date; average delivery time is 12.5 days.
* Why it matters: the estimated delivery date is the customer's explicit
  service expectation, and roughly 1 in 12 delivered orders misses it.
  Whether lateness also drives dissatisfaction cannot be confirmed without
  review data, which this pipeline does not ingest.
* What to do: break the 7,826 late orders down by seller state, customer state,
  category and month to see whether lateness is structural (e.g., long
  SP-to-North/Northeast routes) or seller-specific. Ingesting the geolocation
  and review datasets (present in the raw source, not in this pipeline) would
  make this analysis possible. Only then consider adjusting estimated delivery
  dates; the data here shows the gap, not the cause.

G.2 Manage the catalog by category role: traffic drivers vs revenue drivers
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: cama_mesa_banho leads units (11,115 at avg R$ 93.30) while
  beleza_saude leads revenue (R$ 1,258,681.34 at avg R$ 130.16); the top 10
  categories hold 62.36% of revenue; high-price categories (pcs, avg
  R$ 1,098.34) are niche (203 units).
* Why it matters: treating all categories with one strategy wastes the two
clearly different roles in the mix.
* What to do: evaluate merchandising, promotions and search placement per
  category role; test whether high-volume low-ticket categories can be used
  to attach or cross-sell higher-ticket items. Note that revenue is not
  margin: no cost data exists, so validate against profitability before
  acting.

G.3 Build a proper repeat-purchase and retention view
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: only 3.12% of the 96,096 unique customers ordered more than once in
  the two-year window, yet they contribute 5.73% of revenue.
* Why it matters: if the low repeat rate is real (not an artefact of the
  customer_id / customer_unique_id design or the finite window), retention is
  the largest available growth lever.
* What to do: run a cohort analysis by first-purchase month to see whether
  repeat behaviour improved as the marketplace matured; test post-purchase
  and category-driven re-marketing on high-propensity segments (e.g.,
  consumable categories such as beleza_saude). Confirm first that identity
  linking is reliable before spending on retention programmes.

G.4 Prepare operations for the Black Friday demand spike
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: 24 November 2017 was the busiest day in the dataset (1,176 orders,
  roughly 2.4x the next day), inside the peak month of 7,544 orders.
* Why it matters: a single-day spike stresses picking, carrier handover and
  delivery promises; late deliveries cluster when capacity is exceeded
  (hypothesis to verify).
* What to do: check November 2017 late-delivery rates against other months
  with the existing Gold tables; use the spike to size seasonal capacity and
  delivery-date buffers for promotional events. One observed season does not
  prove a recurring seasonal pattern - only one November exists in the data.

G.5 Use freight visibility in pricing and checkout decisions
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: freight is R$ 2,251,909.54, 14.21% of total order value.
* Why it matters: at this share, freight materially affects basket economics
  and possibly purchase decisions (the dataset cannot show cart abandonment).
* What to do: analyse freight as a share of order value by category and
  customer state (available in the Gold tables) to find where freight is
  heaviest relative to basket; consider free-shipping thresholds or
  seller-state-to-customer-state routing. Any impact on demand must be tested,
  not assumed.

G.6 Balance seller geographic concentration risk
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: 64.40% of revenue comes from SP-based sellers; SP, PR and MG together
  hold 81.12%; BA shows outsized per-seller revenue (R$ 15,029.56 average vs
  SP's R$ 4,734.12) driven by one seller.
* Why it matters: heavy concentration in one state is an operational risk
  (carrier disruption in SP affects two-thirds of revenue) and may lengthen
  delivery distances to other regions - a plausible, untested link to the
  late deliveries in G.1.
* What to do: measure delivery time and lateness by seller-state to
  customer-state pair using the existing Gold tables; if distance-related
  lateness appears, investigate seller onboarding in other regions. No claim
  of seller quality differences is made from revenue alone.

G.7 Close the data-quality gaps that blur the KPIs
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Data: 775 orders (0.78%) have no item records (603 unavailable, 164
  canceled, 5 created, 2 invoiced, 1 shipped); 8 delivered orders lack the
  customer delivery date; 610 of 32,951 products have no category; 1.32% of
  revenue is uncategorised.
* Why it matters: every unclassified or item-less order is revenue, volume
  or delivery performance that the dashboard cannot see.
* What to do: trace the 775 item-less orders upstream in the Bronze/Silver
  layers to determine whether they are genuine cancellations or ingestion
  gaps; add pipeline expectations (row counts, null checks on delivery dates
  and categories) so these populations are monitored rather than discovered
  by hand.


================================================================================
H. DATA LIMITATIONS AND ASSUMPTIONS
================================================================================

1. Historical dataset, not live data. The analysis covers purchases from
   4 September 2016 to 17 October 2018. It says nothing about current
   performance, and no external context (market conditions, marketing
   spend, promotions other than Black Friday) is available.

2. Incomplete months at the edges. 2016-09 (4 orders), 2016-11 (0 orders),
   2016-12 (1 order), 2018-09 (16 orders) and 2018-10 (4 orders, ending
   17 October) are partial months. Monthly-trend statements in this document
   are based on the full months January 2017 - August 2018.

3. 775 orders without matching order-item records (0.78% of 99,441 orders).
   Their order statuses are: unavailable 603, canceled 164, created 5,
   invoiced 2, shipped 1. They have NULL price, freight and item values, so
   they are excluded from revenue, freight, order-value, average-order-value,
   category, product, customer-spending and seller metrics (98,666 orders
   carry item data). They ARE counted in total order counts and status
   distribution.

4. Eight delivered orders excluded from delivery classification. These 8
   delivered orders (out of 96,478) have no order_delivered_customer_date;
   none are missing the estimated delivery date. The on-time/late percentages
   use the 96,470 delivered orders with both dates as the denominator. The
   average of 12.5 delivery days is computed over the same 96,470 orders.

5. 676 customers have NULL total spending. Every order they placed is one of
   the 775 item-less orders, so no spending can be attributed to them. Customer
   spending averages and bands in Section E cover the 95,420 customers with
   measurable spending.

6. Customer identity. Analysis uses customer_unique_id (96,096 unique
   customers). The 99,441 per-order customer_id values would overstate the
   customer count. Repeat-rate caveats are in Section E.4.

7. No margin, cost, payment, review or geolocation data. The pipeline ingested
   only the orders, order_items, products, customers and sellers datasets;
   payments, reviews, geolocation and the category-name translation file
   remain un-ingested. Profitability, customer satisfaction, shipping
   distance and payment-mix claims cannot be made. Category names are in
   Portuguese as delivered by the source.

8. Incremental processing is a demonstration. The MERGE-based incremental
   flow in 04_validation_optimization_sql writes only to the isolated
   workspace.olist.incremental_orders_demo table. It demonstrates insert and
   update batches (upserts) on this static historical dataset; it is not a
   production change-data-capture (CDC) implementation, and no production
   table is incrementally maintained - the pipeline is rebuilt with full
   overwrites.

9. Revenue versus order value. Revenue excludes freight; total order value and
   average order value include freight. Freight represents 14.21% of total
   order value (R$ 2,251,909.54 of R$ 15,843,553.24); relative to product
   revenue alone (R$ 13,591,643.70), including freight raises the figure by
   16.57%. Using total order value in place of revenue would therefore
   overstate revenue by 16.57%, not 14.21%.

10. Seller metrics reflect revenue and volume only. The 3,095-seller revenue
    distribution says nothing about fulfilment quality, review scores or
    profitability; those datasets are absent.

11. Rounding. Currency figures are rounded to 2 decimal places, percentages
    to 2 decimal places; small rounding differences can occur when re-adding
    rounded components (e.g., status shares sum to 100.00 +/- 0.01).


================================================================================
I. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

Over roughly two years (Sep 2016 - Oct 2018) the Olist marketplace in this
dataset processed 99,441 orders, delivered 97.02% of them, and moved
R$ 13,591,643.70 in product revenue (R$ 15,843,553.24 including freight at
14.21% of order value), at an average order value of R$ 160.58 and an average
delivery time of 12.5 days.

The three most decision-relevant findings are:

1. Service quality: 91.89% of classified delivered orders met the estimated
   delivery date, leaving 8.11% (7,826 orders) late - the clearest candidate
   for operational investigation using the existing Gold tables.

2. Catalog structure: revenue and volume are driven by different category
   groups (beleza_saude and relogios_presentes vs cama_mesa_banho), and the
   top 10 of 73 categories concentrate 62.36% of revenue - the assortment is
   broad but not evenly weighted.

3. Market structure: 96,096 unique customers are 96.88% one-time buyers;
   3,095 sellers are geographically concentrated (64.40% of revenue in SP)
   but not revenue-concentrated (top 10 sellers only 13.15%); the average
   seller earns R$ 4,391.48 over the whole period.

The value of the pipeline itself: a Bronze -> Silver -> Gold medallion flow
built with PySpark, Spark SQL and Delta Lake turned five raw CSV files into
eight governed, queryable Gold tables in Unity Catalog, from which the
two-page dashboard and every figure in this document were produced with
consistent, re-runnable definitions (revenue excluding freight, order value
including it, customer identity by customer_unique_id, delivery classification
on delivered orders with both dates). Each number above was verified against
the Gold tables at the time of writing; the limitations in Section H define
exactly what the data can and cannot support.
